Plan C beats plan B on almost every measure — including battery resilience. Reading “battery >80% by end of solar day” as a hard reservation means holding sun back from the pool, which then has to run on night import. That costs 8.04 SEK more over 72 hours and produces more hours on the floor (5 versus 2), because the imported energy went into the pool rather than the pack. The only thing B wins is the literal 08-10 target: 70% against 25%.

On 2026-08-10 the house physically cannot do all four things: 36.6 kWh of PV against 18.2 kWh of base load leaves ~24.8 kWh of surplus, and battery + pool + hot water + car want ~33 kWh. Something has to give, and which one gives is the decision in architecture §8.2 that has not been made. This is the concrete version of that question.

Why the money difference is small — and where it is not

August has almost no price signal. The Ellevio grid tariff is flat at 0.71 SEK/kWh across all 26 published slots, so the entire spread comes from spot: 0.116–0.211 today, 0.157–0.404 tomorrow. Meanwhile import costs ~0.92 and export earns ~0.24 — a 3.8× gap. So in summer the value is self-consumption and battery resilience, not arbitrage. The arbitrage case appears in winter, when PV is scarce and the spot curve actually moves. This plan should not be judged on the -2.52 SEK it saves over these particular 72 hours.

Verification

All invariants hold
Per-slot energy balance closes within 1 W across all 144 simulated slots · SOC stays inside [5%, 100%] · import never exceeds 13.2 kW · no slot both imports and exports · no slot both charges and discharges · every kWh of requirement is placed (0 unmet).
Model checked against a real day. 2026-08-01: PV 58.8 kWh in, load 40, export 10.4, import 1.5, battery +9.3 — closes to 0.6 kWh of round-trip loss, which is where the 90.25% efficiency figure comes from. Battery drained to its 5% floor before dawn, then exported 10.4 kWh at 17:00-19:00 once full. Both are the behaviours the plan should fix.
